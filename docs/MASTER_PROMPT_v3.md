## A. Product mission and absolute UX principles

This is a simple Indian rental billing, payment tracking, and collection application, initially optimized for small commercial shop owners (e.g., 5–50 shops) but capable of supporting residential rentals and many independent owners. It is **not** a full property-management ERP. Support shops, commercial units, and residential units with the same underlying model; use appropriate labels in the UI. Make the UI usable by owners and tenants with limited technical knowledge.

**One mobile application, not two.** One React Native + Expo + TypeScript codebase published to Android and iOS, with Owner, Tenant, or Both roles. The same user can own/manage properties and rent other properties. Show only role-relevant functionality, with an obvious role switcher for Both. One backend, one database. A web admin portal is optional, not a prerequisite; do not build five separate apps.

**Minimum mandatory input, minimal scrolling, minimal taps.** Use compact screens, large touch targets, bottom tabs, inline validations, searchable selectors, numeric keypads, default values, auto-filled details, short step flows, and progressive disclosure. Avoid huge forms, excessive cards, endless scrolling, and complicated menus. For manual onboarding target two short steps; payment recording target Select Tenant -> Amount -> Confirm. No mandatory information that is not necessary for identity, billing, tenancy linkage, or compliance. Keep optional and advanced features hidden until needed.

## E. Monthly rent, charges, deposits, and accounting

Use a **proper ledger**, not a mutable `paid` boolean. All money in INR using decimal types/paise integers, never floating point. Unique invoice/receipt references, immutable transaction entries, audited corrections/reversals, PostgreSQL transactions and locking where appropriate. Month/year + tenancy scoped billing. Define consistent timezone Asia/Kolkata and billing period handling.

- Recurring monthly rent per tenancy, individual due day, optional start/end date, prorated first/last month, grace periods, rent-free periods, configurable annual rent increases/renewals. For due day 29–31 in shorter months use a documented last-day-of-month rule.
- Separate monthly charge lines for **electricity/power, water, maintenance, miscellaneous**, plus optional other charges. Owner configures which categories apply to each shop; hidden when unused. Owner can enter or modify monthly amounts. Tenant may **enter proposed readings and/or amounts for the applicable month's electricity, water, and miscellaneous charges**, optionally maintenance where configured; owner must approve tenant-entered charges before they become payable. Tenant can see owner-fed values. Show proposed/pending/approved/rejected state, who entered them, timestamps, and optional bill photo. Tenant cannot directly overwrite an owner-approved amount or create an immediately collectible charge. Owner updates to an already issued/paid invoice must create traceable adjustments/credit notes, not silently rewrite history.
- Recurring maintenance can carry forward; utilities can copy last month only as an editable suggestion, not silently bill guessed amounts. Show one compact monthly bill: rent + applicable utilities + maintenance + miscellaneous + past arrears - payments/credits = total due. Break down individual line items without scrolling excessively.
- **Partial payments in any positive amount** through allowed channels, with a clearly defined allocation policy (default oldest outstanding dues first; provide allocation breakdown and configurable per-charge allocation where appropriate). Example: ₹10,000 due, ₹7,000 paid -> ₹3,000 balance; next month ₹10,000 -> ₹13,000 outstanding before new payments. Avoid double counting carry-forward amounts.
- **Cash payments**: authorized owner selects tenant/unit, enters amount/date, confirms, and immediately records an owner-confirmed payment in the ledger; tenant sees the cash receipt and updated balance and gets an eligible notification. Tenant can acknowledge or dispute the record. Cash is explicitly labeled **owner-recorded**, not gateway-verified. Correction through audited reversal/adjustment, not deletion. Owner may optionally attach proof or note.
- **Online payments**: tenant selects full or partial amount, UPI app/intent/QR or other PSP-supported option. Only mark paid after signed, server-verified gateway event or authoritative status check; idempotent webhook processing and settlement reconciliation. A screenshot or manual UPI transfer is not automatically verified. If supporting offline/manual bank or UPI transfers, mark pending verification until owner confirms/reconciles; distinguish from gateway-confirmed payments.
- **Security deposit/advance**: track as a separate balance, never mix with rental income. Owner records deposit, can apply an authorized, audited amount against dues, tenant sees every adjustment and can dispute. Example ₹45,000 deposit - ₹15,000 approved adjustment = ₹30,000 remaining. At exit, calculate outstanding charges, approved deductions, refunds and final statement. Keep receipt and refund evidence. Do not let AI adjust deposit autonomously.
- **Tax handling**: optional configurable GST/TDS fields and reports for Indian commercial rental scenarios, but never assume universal rates or automatically deduct tax without validated current rules and user configuration. Include tax invoice/receipt information where applicable. Tax/compliance workflows should be reviewed by qualified India specialists before production.
- Opening unpaid balances for existing tenants; manual adjustments with reason and audit; month-by-month paid/partial/unpaid/overdue statuses; receipts and downloadable statements (PDF) in selected language when supported.
